### ECS-2026-10-14-D5-01: projectile and `ProcessTrapHit` damage is produced after `combat_damage_system` and `hit_event_system`, then drained by the Late cleanup, so it never lands
- **Severity**: MEDIUM
- **Dimension**: Scheduler wiring (cross-stage / same-stage producer-after-consumer, invisible to the access analyzer)
- **Location**: `byroredux/src/boot/schedule/update.rs:400` (`combat_damage_system`), `:550` (`hit_event_system`), `:593` (`projectile_flight_system`); `crates/scripting/src/fragment/effects.rs:827-845` (the `ProcessTrapHit` flush); `crates/scripting/src/cleanup.rs:94` (`drain_component::<HitEvent>`); `byroredux/src/boot/schedule/late.rs:821` (`event_cleanup_system`)
- **Status**: NEW (fixed in the commit that follows this report: `combat_damage_system` and `hit_event_system` now register after the projectile flight, and `hit_event_consumers_run_after_every_producer` pins it)
- **Description**: `HitEvent` is a one-frame marker: `combat_damage_system` consumes it for health, death and bleedout, `hit_event_system` delivers it to scripts that registered with `RegisterForHitEvent`, and `event_cleanup_system` (Late) drains whatever is left. The melee, NPC-strike and force-greet producers all run before `combat_damage_system` in the Update stage (`update.rs` ~`:392-400`). The two M47.2 producers do not:
  - `projectile_flight_system` is registered at `update.rs:593` and inserts a `HitEvent` on every hit actor.
  - `ProcessTrapHit` is recorded by the Papyrus VM and flushed into `HitEvent` markers by `DeferredFragmentEffects` (`effects.rs:827-845`). That flush runs inside the reference/alias/quest script systems, all of which sit at `update.rs:540-545`, after `combat_damage_system`.
  Both markers are therefore inserted after the damage consumer (`:400`) and after the script hit dispatcher (`:550`) for that frame, and `event_cleanup_system` drains them at the end of the same frame. Neither consumer ever sees them. `projectile_launch_system` and `trap_contact_system` have a one-frame delivery latency by design (their events go through `PendingUnloadDeliveries`), but a `HitEvent` is a marker, not a queue, so it has no second frame.
- **Evidence**: registration order in `update.rs`: `combat_damage_system` at `:400`, `reference_script_event_system` at `:545`, `hit_event_system` at `:550`, `trap_contact_system` / `projectile_launch_system` / `projectile_flight_system` at `:591-593`. `combat_damage_system` has exactly one production registration. No test pins the order: `flight_into_an_actor_queues_a_hit_and_into_a_wall_only_ends` calls `projectile_flight_system` directly and asserts the marker exists, and the scheduler ordering tests (`scheduler_access_tests.rs:1030-1070`) only pin that `combat_damage_system` declares access. This matches the session's own note that a dart or blade actually hurting a target was never verified live.
- **Impact**: `Weapon.Fire` projectiles and Skyrim trap scripts that call `ProcessTrapHit` (the dart trap, the swinging blade) deal no damage, cause no death or bleedout, raise no KILL story event, and never reach `RegisterForHitEvent` listeners. The trap contact events, sounds and animation all work, which is why the traps look finished.
- **Suggested Fix**: either register the projectile and VM-effect producers ahead of `combat_damage_system` (the projectile systems have no dependency on later Update systems; the script flush is the harder case), or move `combat_damage_system` and `hit_event_system` after the script and projectile systems, so that they consume what the same frame produced. Add a scheduler-order test that pins "every `HitEvent` producer registers before `combat_damage_system` and `hit_event_system`" by name, like `fragment_activation_order_tests`, and a headless test that drives a projectile hit through the real schedule and asserts the victim lost health.

### ECS-2026-10-14-D7-02: `trap_contact_system` loses its contact set when `PhysicsWorld` is missing and re-fires `OnTrapHitStart`
- **Severity**: LOW
- **Dimension**: Component lifecycles (transient state)
- **Location**: `byroredux/src/systems/trap_hits.rs:144-163`
- **Status**: NEW
- **Description**: The system takes the previous contacts with `std::mem::take(&mut contacts.0)`, and the later `let Some(physics) = world.try_resource::<PhysicsWorld>() else { return };` leaves `TrapContacts` empty. Any actor still standing in a trap then looks like a new contact the next time the world exists and gets `OnTrapHitStart` and `OnTrapHit` again.
- **Evidence**: `previous` is taken at `:144-147`; the early return at `:161-163` does not write it back.
- **Impact**: latent only. `traps` is non-empty only when `RapierHandles` rows exist, which implies a `PhysicsWorld`, so the branch needs the resource to disappear mid-run.
- **Suggested Fix**: write `previous` back before returning, or take the resource first and the contacts second.
